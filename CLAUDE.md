# CLAUDE.md

Splatacama is a Next.js 15 app (App Router, React 19, Tailwind 4) that shows archaeological sites in the Atacama Desert on a CesiumJS 3D globe. Each site opens a Gaussian splat viewer (Spark, `@sparkjsdev/spark`, on three.js) with terrain to the horizon, scene effects and VR. See [README.md](README.md) for setup, environment variables, and the file layout.

## Commands

- `pnpm dev`, `pnpm build`, `pnpm start`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm test`, `pnpm typecheck`. Use pnpm; the lockfile is pnpm v9 format.
- `pnpm lint` and `pnpm typecheck` should report 0 problems (TypeScript is strict). `pnpm build` type-checks and lints too, so either kind of error fails the Vercel deploy. `pnpm test` runs Vitest on the pure logic: asset URLs, pin grouping and fan-out, and the site data (`landmarks.json`, `windTurbines.json`). Vercel doesn't run the tests, so run them before pushing.
- After changing anything the map loads, test a **production** build (`pnpm build && pnpm start`), not only `pnpm dev`: the dev server doesn't minify, and one minifier bug has already broken production only (see the SPZ gotcha below).
- Runtime needs `NEXT_PUBLIC_CESIUM_TOKEN` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_R2_PUBLIC_DOMAIN` in `.env.local`. Without the Cesium token the map shows an error instead of loading.
- Vercel refuses to deploy Next.js versions with known critical advisories. Keep Next.js patched.

## Architecture in brief

- **Data:** [src/data/landmarks.json](src/data/landmarks.json) is the only site list, imported statically by the map and by the scene page. The scene pages are prebuilt with `generateStaticParams`; unknown ids return a 404.
- **Assets:** `.splat` files, terrain (`.glb`), skies, preview JPEGs and pin thumbnails live in Cloudflare R2. `assetUrl()` in [src/utils/assetUrl.ts](src/utils/assetUrl.ts) turns a bucket path into `https://<R2 domain>/<path>`, and the browser downloads it directly. There's no server code; every page is static.
- **Map:** `MapWithPins` loads `CesiumMapComponent` with `ssr: false`. `useCesiumViewer` builds the viewer with ArcGIS World Imagery and Cesium World Terrain selected in the constructor, one billboard entity per landmark, pin clustering, and click and hover handlers. The effect returns a teardown that destroys all of it. Pin images are canvases (a round crop of `thumbnailUrl`, falling back to `previewUrl`, then an orange circle) cached under `${id}_img` or `${id}_fallback`.
- **Map to scene:** the "Ver Escena 3D" button does a full page load (`window.location.href`), not client-side routing.
- **Splat viewer:** [src/components/GSplatViewer.tsx](src/components/GSplatViewer.tsx) builds a three.js renderer, a Spark `SparkRenderer` and three's `OrbitControls` in a container React doesn't manage; the effect's teardown disposes all of it. It downloads the `.splat` itself (and the terrain alongside) to drive its Spanish progress panel, and hands Spark the bytes with an explicit `fileType: SplatFileType.SPLAT`. The scene is y-down (`camera.up` [0, -1, 0]) and the splat is scaled 10× (`SCENE_SCALE`); everything drawn for the site sits in one `world` group in that frame.
- **Terrain:** pirca11–13 are splats cropped to the site (the photos saw the ground farther out only edge-on) with `environment` in `landmarks.json`: a terrain `.glb` out to 25 km and a sky, built by the splat pipeline outside the repo (`~/datasets/pircas-work`, see its README). [src/utils/sceneEnvironment.ts](src/utils/sceneEnvironment.ts) loads them. The terrain's `terrain_under*` nodes, under the splat, don't write depth and come after the rest, so the splat covers them. Its materials carry `extras.detail`: a tiling texture of the site's real ground that `addTerrainDetail` multiplies in from the splat's edge to 2 km, kept off the sea and steep slopes. Near the site the terrain's texture can be a mosaic of the site's own drone photos (the second batch; the pipeline's `photo_mosaic`), with its coverage in the glb as `extras.detail.photoMask`: there the shader leaves out the scrub map, the mottling, the gullies and the slope shading (`beyondPhoto`), since the photos have the real ones. The terrain is unlit in the file; the shader lights its slopes with the site's `sun` (relative to level ground, under the baked haze), adds rock-and-sand mottling the 10 m satellite colours can't show and gullies down the slopes (`terrainGullies`), keeps pale dune sand a warm sand colour, and draws the sea itself: moving waves reflecting the sky (a tiling wave-slope texture at four scales), the sun's glitter, turquoise by the shore, and white water as lace (a tiling foam texture; both in `public/textures/`, made by the pipeline's `sea_textures.py`): surf as breaking lines running in parallel to the shore (where the satellite saw white water, and gentler along every shore), whitecaps (no foam is drawn along the water's edge itself: a band by mesh distance covered flat shores in still white lace, and a thin line at the water's height read as a static drawn line), and the shoreline: a sharp water's edge placed in metres along the ground from the height above the sea over the smooth slope (so it doesn't depend on the mesh's wide shore triangles), bent by noise and moved by the swash, with wet sand above it and the sand showing through the shallows (`prepareTerrainGeometry` works out each vertex's distance from the land and that white water when the terrain loads). `sceneEffects.ts` sets its sun and swell (`environment.light`). The sky sphere's shader (`SKY_FRAGMENT`) and the terrain's share `SKY_LIGHT_GLSL`: a drifting cumulus layer (`cloudCover`), whose shadows cross the land past 80 m from the site and which the sea reflects, the sun's disc, and haze brighter and warmer toward the sun.
- **Camera limits:** any angle, but no farther than `view.maxDistance` from the orbit centre, never more than `view.radius` from the site's centre (about where the cropped splat starts to fade), the orbit centre within half that, and never below a standing visitor's eye height (`EYE_HEIGHT`) or 0.5 m above walls and bushes, read from the site analysis. The user asked for exactly that (2026-11-08): no angle limits.
- **Camera limits:** distance only (`view.maxDistance`, `minDistance`, `radius`) and staying above the ground. Never limit the camera's angle (the user's rule). A site that looks best from above sets `view.startElevation`, which only places the start and reset camera; `view.vr: false` hides the VR button.
- **360 photo:** a site with `pano` in `landmarks.json` (the drone's panorama, registered in the site's reconstruction by the pipeline's `pano_register.py` and `pano_site.py`) gets a "Foto 360°" button. [src/utils/panoView.ts](src/utils/panoView.ts) flies the camera to the point the photo was taken at and offers three views from there: "Foto 360°" (the photo on a sphere around the camera, turned as registered, where the camera only turns and zooms), "Foto 3D" (with `pano.depth`: the photo on a mesh with every pixel at its distance, from a depth map estimated by the Depth Any Panoramas model and scaled to the reconstruction's metres by the pipeline's `pano_depth.py` and `pano_depth_fit.py`; "Mapa de profundidad" colours it by distance) and "Escena 3D" (the reconstruction). In the last two the camera can move up to `depth.reach` metres from the viewpoint (wheel, W A S D Q E, right-drag, two fingers), and the offset is kept when switching between them, so the two can be compared from the same displaced point; the photo pulls it back. Photo and relief are drawn in a pass of their own over the scene's, and while they cover the frame the scene isn't drawn. The orbit controls are off at the viewpoint and their limits don't apply; leaving puts the camera back. The relief is one photo pushed into shape: gaps behind things are stretched colours, which is why the reach is short. The model's licence is CC BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial, with the credit the viewer shows). If a site's splat is rebuilt with another centre or heading, rerun `pano_site.py`: the entry is in the viewer's frame.
- **Photo poses:** a site with `photoPosesUrl` in `landmarks.json` gets a "Cámaras de las fotos" switch in the Efectos panel. [src/utils/photoPoses.ts](src/utils/photoPoses.ts) draws every photo the site was reconstructed from as a wireframe pyramid (tip at the camera, base the picture's frame), as COLMAP's viewer does, and the 360 photo's viewpoint as blue rings. The poses are a small JSON file in the bucket, written in the viewer's frame by the pipeline's `site_cameras.py` from the model the live splat was trained from; rerun it if a site's splat is rebuilt. The lines are three's fat lines (meshes), drawn with the terrain before the splats. pirca0 has none (its photos aren't kept).
- **Scene versions:** a site with `variants` in `landmarks.json` (only Pirka 10 so far: its uncropped splat, "Solo splat", and that splat trained with the 360 photo's views and depth as well, "Splat + 360°") gets a switch between the scene as the site has it ("Con terreno": the cropped splat with the terrain mesh, the sky and the plants) and each variant, a splat that carries its own surroundings and is shown without them. The switch is at the bottom centre in the free view and, at the 360 photo's viewpoint, takes the place of "Escena 3D" next to "Foto 360°" and "Foto 3D", so all of them can be compared from one point. A variant's splat is downloaded when first chosen; `SceneEffects.setSiteShown` hides the site's own splat, terrain and plants meanwhile. Night, fog and VR act on the site's own version only (entering VR goes back to it).
- **Trial address flags:** `?splat=<file in the bucket>` loads that splat in place of the site's and `?noenv` leaves out the terrain mesh, the sky and the plants, to compare versions of a scene on the live site without changing it for visitors (for example `/scene/pirca2-10?splat=pirca2-10_full.splat&noenv`, the uncropped splat with its own surroundings).
- **Graphics settings:** the Efectos panel's "Gráficos" part ([graphicsSettings.ts](src/utils/graphicsSettings.ts), remembered in `localStorage`): Nubes (`environment.light.clouds`), Luz atmosférica (`light.glow`), Bruma costera (a light camanchaca: `createCamanchaca` with `FOG_WISPS`, faint wisps thinned into drifting patches, plus `light.mist`, banks of mist low over the sea and the coast in the terrain shader) and Aves marinas ([seabirds.ts](src/effects/seabirds.ts): gulls wheeling and a file of pelicans, one instanced mesh of a few triangles each, wingbeats in the vertex shader, never nearer than 30 m to the site; hidden at night). Calidad alta (`quality`, off by default, hidden on headsets and never active in VR) is the mode for computers that trades frame rate for looks: `light.quality` switches the sky from the flat cloud layer to clouds with depth (`SKY_VOLUME_GLSL`: a ray march through the same layer, lit toward the sun), which `createVolumeClouds` draws before the scene each frame into an image of at most `CLOUD_IMAGE_PIXELS` that the sky shader lays over the panorama (`environment.updateClouds`, called from `sceneEffects.ts`); the clouds' shadows then cross the site too, the terrain's own near the site and the same shadow on the splats (`createCloudShade`, from `cloudShadowField`, the shader's cloud noise worked out on the CPU), at the strength of the site's `sun.shadows`; and the meshes get 4 samples before FXAA (`meshAntialias.setSamples`). Anything that changes the cloud layer's noise must change `cloudShadowField` with it.
- **Effects:** the "Efectos" panel ([SceneEffectsPanel.tsx](src/components/SceneEffectsPanel.tsx)) drives [src/effects/sceneEffects.ts](src/effects/sceneEffects.ts): night (real stars and Milky Way for the site's place and time, the scene darkened, turbine lights), and camanchaca (fog splats drifting inland plus three.js fog on the terrain). People who walk the site ([npc.ts](src/effects/npc.ts), `npcs` in `landmarks.json`) live there too, on a walkable grid made from the same analysis. They all build on [siteAnalysis.ts](src/effects/siteAnalysis.ts), a 2.5D reading of the splats (ground, walls, bushes) done when the scene loads, in a "metric" frame: metres, y up. A group turns and scales that frame into the viewer's. Ported from a standalone prototype lab outside the repo (`~/datasets/pircas-work/lab`).
- **VR:** on a headset (or a computer with one attached) the viewer offers "Ver en VR". [src/utils/walkArea.ts](src/utils/walkArea.ts) reads where a visitor can walk off the splats when the scene loads (about 60 ms): ground height, obstacles (walls, bushes) and the edge of the well-captured area, on a 20 cm grid, tuned in `WALK_CONFIG`. [src/utils/vrWalk.ts](src/utils/vrWalk.ts) runs the WebXR session: the `world` group is turned upright (180° about x) and scaled to metres, the camera sits in a rig that the left stick moves and the right stick turns in steps, and the head, including walking around the room, is held inside the walk area. Exiting restores the desktop view. Spark's level-of-detail tree (`lod`) is built only when VR is offered, because Spark draws at most 500k splats per frame on a Quest. A headset's GPU draws each pixel many times slower than a computer's (on a Quest 3 the terrain and sky cost as much as the splats), so while a session runs `SceneEffects.setHeadset` turns on `light.lite` (no gullies or rock mottling, simpler cloud shadows and mist) and hides the Bruma costera wisps, and a headset's browser gets 4-sample multisampling of the whole VR image (`antialias: isHeadsetBrowser()`). `meshAntialias.renderXR` multisamples only the meshes instead, which should be cheaper; it runs only with `?vrmesh` in the address until it has been seen working on a real headset (its first version drew no splats on the Quest, which the emulator didn't show).
- **Sound and turbines:** [sceneSound.ts](src/utils/sceneSound.ts) makes the sound in the browser, with no recordings: wind from noise in gusts ([windAmbience.ts](src/utils/windAmbience.ts); the recording it replaced was mostly road noise), and the eight nearest turbines' whine and blade swish, each positioned at its hub and heard from the camera ([turbineSound.ts](src/utils/turbineSound.ts)). It starts on the first click or key; [SceneSound.tsx](src/components/SceneSound.tsx) is the sound button, whose panel turns each sound on or off (remembered in the browser; with all off the audio context is suspended). [src/utils/windTurbines.ts](src/utils/windTurbines.ts) draws the real wind turbines in [windTurbines.json](src/data/windTurbines.json) with turning rotors; only pirca0 has any in view. The model is procedural (tapered tower, twisted and pitched blades with red-white-red tips) and lit to match pirca0's photo. On a site with terrain they stand on it at their real distances (the pipeline's `env_build.py --turbines` writes them); on a full splat without terrain they'd be a backdrop on its horizon, fitted to renders (README, "Wind turbines").

## Gotchas

- **Keep webpack, not Turbopack.** [next.config.js](next.config.js) aliases `cesium` to its package path (a pnpm workaround), defines `CESIUM_BASE_URL=/cesium` (the only place it's set), and copies Cesium's Workers, ThirdParty, Assets and Widgets folders into `public/cesium/` (gitignored). `next dev --turbopack` would skip all of that and break Cesium.
- **SPZ loader alias.** Cesium 1.145 depends on `@spz-loader/core`, whose embedded WebAssembly is turned into invalid JavaScript by the production minifier, so Cesium fails to load. `next.config.js` aliases it to `false`. Keep that alias unless a later Cesium or Next.js fixes it; check with a production build.
- **The map only redraws on request.** `VIEWER_CONFIG.requestRenderMode` is on. Camera moves and tile loads trigger redraws automatically, but entity changes don't. Any code that changes pins, clusters or other entities must call `viewer.scene.requestRender()`, or the change won't show until the camera moves.
- **R2's CORS rule decides where assets load.** The browser fetches splats and pin photos from the bucket directly, and pin photos are drawn into WebGL, which needs CORS. The rule allows `https://splatacama.vercel.app` and `http://localhost:3000` only. On a Vercel preview deployment or another local port, scenes fail to load and pins stay orange until that origin is added in the Cloudflare dashboard.
- **Cesium runs in the browser only.** Anything that touches `cesium` or `window` must stay behind the `ssr: false` dynamic import or inside `useEffect`.
- **Pin heights are relative to the ground.** Pins, cluster markers and the explode dot use `HeightReference.RELATIVE_TO_GROUND`, and `height` in `landmarks.json` is meters above the ground. Terrain is exaggerated 1.5× (`SCENE_CONFIG.verticalExaggeration`); absolute heights put pins underground.
- **Overlapping pins.** `pirca11` and `pirca12` are 30 m apart, so their pins stack at almost every zoom level. [src/utils/cesiumPinClusters.ts](src/utils/cesiumPinClusters.ts) groups pins closer than `MARKER_CONFIG.clusterDistance` on screen, every frame. Each group gets a cluster entity with id `cluster:<ids>`, drawn as a stack of its pins' images plus a count badge (`createClusterMarker`). Clicking one fans its pins out with `billboard.pixelOffset`. Use `pinClusters.getClusterMembers(entity)` to recognize a picked cluster.
- **Don't switch back to Cesium's `EntityCluster`.** It deletes and recreates cluster markers on every camera change, and their images reload asynchronously, so clusters blinked out while zooming. For the same reason, pins under a cluster are shrunk to width 0 instead of `show = false`, and a dissolving cluster's marker is removed one frame after its pins reappear. A probe that zooms the map and counts frames where a site isn't drawn lives outside the repo, at `~/datasets/pircas-work/viewer-test/zoom_probe.py` on the machine that built the splats. It should report 0.
- **Render resolution.** `setupViewerSettings` turns off `useBrowserRecommendedResolution` and caps rendering at 2× device pixels. Marker canvases are drawn at `MARKER_CONFIG.textureScale` so they match. Together these fix blurry, jagged pins on high-density screens, at some GPU cost.
- **Frames are drawn in three passes** ([meshAntialias.ts](src/utils/meshAntialias.ts)): the meshes (terrain, sky, turbines) into a texture, then to the screen through FXAA with their depth, then the splats over them with every other drawable hidden. The renderer has no multisampling (Spark's advice: it multiplies the cost of every splat), so this is what smooths mesh edges, for one full-screen pass. VR draws in one pass, without it. Anything new drawn in the scene is smoothed with the meshes unless it's a splat.
- **Spark draws splats after all opaque meshes, and they don't write depth.** Opaque meshes (terrain, turbines) hide splats behind them; splats in front blend over them. Turbines on a full splat without terrain would stand behind its splat sky, so there they write their depth as if 40 units away (`DEPTH_DISTANCE`). Either way they're drawn before the splats, so they can't be semi-transparent, and the renderer's missing antialiasing shows on them: blades and towers thinner than a pixel would break into dots. The turbine shader widens them to 1.5 pixels and fades them toward `sky` in `windTurbines.json` by how much they fall short. If pirca0's terrain is rebuilt, re-run `env_build.py --turbines` (README, "Wind turbines").
- **Spark drops scale from the camera.** It decomposes the camera's matrix and ignores scale, so a scaled rig breaks rendering. VR shows real size by scaling the `world` group instead.
- **Spark takes the bytes it's given.** They move to its worker, so the viewer gives it a copy: the site analysis reads the original `.splat` bytes.
- **Meshes in the scene must not be blended.** A transparent material is drawn in three.js's transparent pass, alongside Spark's splats, which don't write depth: it would show through walls. npc.ts turns the characters' blended materials (hair, eyelashes) into cut-outs (`alphaTest`).
- **Night and fog hide the far splats.** Pirca 3's splat keeps a dome of sky splats far out (and a full splat without terrain would have its sky on a shell); `siteSplatModifier` in `sceneEffects.ts` fades them out so daytime sky doesn't show at night or in fog.
- **Three.js fog is always on.** `scene.fog` is set once and pushed far away when the camanchaca is off, because adding or removing it recompiles every terrain shader.
- **GLSL reserved words.** The terrain shader is injected into three's; a variable named like a GLSL keyword (`flat` and `half` broke it) fails to compile only in the browser. Check the console after changing it.
- **Testing VR without a headset.** Meta's WebXR emulator (`iwer`, with `installRuntime({ forceInstall: true })` on desktop Chrome) emulates a Quest 3 with scriptable thumbsticks; it passes for a Quest, so Spark uses its headset splat budget. Real headset performance still needs a real headset.
- **Default view.** The start camera (`DEFAULT_CAMERA_CONFIG.center`, looking northeast) doesn't show `pirca11` and `pirca12`, which are about 16 km southeast.
- **Splat blur.** Brush trains splats with a 0.3 px blur and no opacity change; Spark's default blur is anti-aliased and fades small splats, so some models come out pale in the app though they look right in a plain viewer. `plainBlur: true` in a site's `landmarks.json` entry draws it with the trainer's blur (set for the sites retrained on 18 Nov 2026: `pirca2-04`, `-07`, `-08`). Compare any site both ways with `?blur=plain` and `?blur=aa` before changing it: the earlier models of Pirka 8 and 11 turned paler with `plainBlur`.

## Conventions

- Text users see is in Spanish (for example "Volver al mapa", "Cargando mapa 3D..."). Code, comments, and identifiers are in English.
- Styling is Tailwind 4 utility classes inline. Use the slash syntax for opacity (`bg-gray-900/90`); the Tailwind 3 `bg-opacity-*` utilities don't exist here. UI panels use dark gray with orange accents (`bg-gray-900/90`, `orange-600`).
- Import from `src/` with the `@/` alias.
- Cesium and viewer objects are typed `any` throughout, and ESLint's `no-explicit-any` is off for that reason. Match that instead of adding partial typings.
